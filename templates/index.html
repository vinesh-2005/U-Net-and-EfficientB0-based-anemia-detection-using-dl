🥗 Anemia Awareness & Nutritional Guidance Health Education

Comprehensive, evidence-based dietary recommendations to support healthy hemoglobin levels, optimize dietary iron absorption, and maintain red blood cell production.

🩺 A. Steps to Support Healthy Hemoglobin Levels
1

Include Iron-Rich Foods

Incorporate both heme and non-heme iron sources in daily meals to replenish body iron stores.

2

Consume Vitamin C-Rich Foods

Vitamin C converts dietary ferric iron into easily absorbable ferrous iron when consumed with meals.

3

Include Folate & Vitamin B12 Sources

Folate (B9) and Cobalamin (B12) are critical cofactors for red blood cell synthesis and DNA repair.

4

Maintain a Balanced Diet

Ensure adequate dietary protein, essential trace minerals (copper, zinc), and proper daily hydration.

5

Consult a Healthcare Professional

Visit a qualified physician to identify the underlying cause of anemia (iron loss, chronic disease, or malabsorption).

6

Follow Medically Prescribed Treatment

Adhere strictly to clinical recommendations, prescribed therapies, and routine follow-up blood tests.

⚠️ C. What to Limit
☕

Limit Tea & Coffee Near Meals

Tannins and polyphenols found in coffee and black/green tea bind tightly to non-heme iron and inhibit absorption. Avoid having tea or coffee within 1 to 2 hours of iron-rich meals.

💊

Avoid Unsupervised Self-Medication

Do not initiate high-dose iron supplements without diagnostic clinical blood tests. Excessive unmanaged iron stores can accumulate in organs and cause toxicity.

🥛

Separate Calcium from Iron

Calcium directly competes with iron across intestinal transport channels. Calcium supplements or large dairy portions should be spaced a few hours apart from iron supplements as medically advised.
